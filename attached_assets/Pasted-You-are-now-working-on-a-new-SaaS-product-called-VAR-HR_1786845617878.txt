You are now working on a new SaaS product called VAR HR.

VAR HR is a premium SaaS platform for companies to manage their workforce, attendance, leave, permissions, biometric devices, payroll, attendance rules, employee records, reports, and subscriptions.

The product must feel like a serious, premium, enterprise-grade platform from the first interaction.

This is NOT intended to be a basic attendance system or a generic admin dashboard.

The goal is to build a product that looks and feels expensive, mature, reliable, scalable, and professionally designed.

==================================================
1. PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY
==================================================

There is a principle behind this project:

"We start where others stopped."

This does NOT mean copying existing HR products.

You should understand the strongest HR/HCM products in the market and study:

- What problems they solve.
- Why their workflows are designed the way they are.
- Their strengths.
- Their weaknesses.
- Where users experience friction.
- What can be simplified.
- What can be improved.
- What can be redesigned.
- What should be approached completely differently.

Then build VAR HR around its own product philosophy and design language.

DO NOT copy existing products.

Do not copy:

- UI
- Layouts
- Branding
- Visual identity
- Copywriting
- Components
- Exact workflows
- Navigation structures
- Design patterns in a way that makes VAR HR look derivative

Market research is encouraged for understanding products, problems, workflows, and industry standards.

The final product must have its own identity and its own solutions.

The objective is:

Understand what already exists.
Understand why it exists.
Identify what can be improved.
Then build VAR HR with our own thinking and design decisions.

4. GPS ATTENDANCE
==================================================

VAR HR must support attendance based on the employee's current location.

- Capture location when checking out.

==================================================

↕
Biometric Devices

The system must be designed from the beginning to support multiple biometric device manufacturers and models.

DO NOT assume that every biometric device uses the same protocol.

Create a clean abstraction/integration architecture that allows different device connectors or adapters to be added without redesigning the entire platform.

The platform should eventually support:

- Adding biometric devices.
- Assigning devices to companies.
- Assigning devices to branches.
- Connecting devices.
- Disconnecting devices.
- Synchronizing devices.
- Automatically pulling attendance records.
- Synchronizing employees.
- Adding employees to biometric devices from VAR HR.
- Updating employee information on biometric devices.
- Managing multiple biometric devices for one company.
- Supporting different device manufacturers.
- Supporting different device protocols through adapters/connectors.
- Monitoring synchronization status.
- Showing connection errors.
- Showing last synchronization time.
- Showing device health/status.

The desired workflow is:

Company Owner
→ Employees
→ Select Biometric Device
→ Sync Employee
→ Employee becomes available on device

And:

Biometric Device
→ Attendance Record
→ VAR HR
→ Attendance Engine
→ Rules Engine
→ Payroll

The user should NOT have to manually export attendance spreadsheets and upload them as the primary workflow.

Manual import can still exist as a fallback.

- Working hours.
- Grace period.
- Late threshold.
- Early departure rules.
- Overtime rules.
- Overtime start conditions.
- Working days.
- Holidays.
- Shifts.
- Different rules for branches.
- Different rules for departments.
- Different rules for employees when necessary.

The system should automatically calculate:

Attendance
→ Late
→ Early Leave
→ Absence
→ Overtime
→ Deductions
→ Additions

Calculations must be explainable.

Do not show only a final number.

For example:

Basic Salary: 10,000
Overtime: +500
Late Deductions: -150
Absence Deductions: -300
Other Additions: +200

Net Salary: 10,250

The user must be able to understand exactly how the system arrived at the final amount.

20. RTL / LTR
==================================================

Arabic must support full RTL.

English, French, and German must support LTR.

When Arabic is selected:

- The entire interface must switch to RTL.
- Navigation must correctly mirror.
- Sidebars must correctly mirror.
- Forms must correctly align.
- Tables must remain readable.
- Directional icons should mirror when appropriate.
- Spacing must remain visually balanced.
- Modals must support RTL.
- Dropdowns must support RTL.
- Charts and data visualizations must remain usable.
- Text alignment must be correct.
- Layouts must not break.

When English, French, or German is selected:

- The interface must switch to LTR.
- The layout must return to the correct LTR structure.

RTL must NOT be treated as a later adaptation.

Arabic must be considered during the original design and component architecture.

==================================================
